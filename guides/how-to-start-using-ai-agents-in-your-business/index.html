How to start using AI agents in your business

By Demetri Panagiotou, founder of PremierConnect AI · November 3, 2026

The short answer: start with one job that repeats every day and costs you money when it is late, like answering new inquiries. Measure it as it is now. Run an agent on that one job, with a person approving what it sends. After 30 days, compare. Then add the next job.

What an AI agent is

A chatbot answers questions in a window. An automation runs the same fixed steps every time. An agent reads what came in, decides what to do within rules you set, and does it across your tools: writes the reply, books the time, updates the record, and reports back.

The first jobs worth giving one

  • After-hours inquiries. Calls and forms that come in at night get an answer in minutes, not the next morning.
  • Lead follow-up. Every lead gets followed up on schedule until it replies.
  • Booking and reminders. Appointments get booked and confirmed, and no-shows get a nudge.
  • Review requests. Happy customers get asked at the right moment.
  • Records. Your CRM stays current without someone typing on Friday afternoon.

What to keep human

  • Prices, discounts and anything you would sign.
  • Complaints and upset customers.
  • Legal, medical, tax or investment questions.
  • At the start, the final send on anything a customer will read.

How to start without risk

  1. Pick one job. Not five.
  2. Count it today. How many inquiries a week, how fast you answer, how many turn into booked work.
  3. Run the agent with approval on. It drafts, a person sends.
  4. Compare after 30 days. Same counts, side by side.
  5. Loosen or widen. Let it send routine replies on its own, or add the next job.

Questions to ask anyone who builds agents for you

  • Can I see every message the agent sent, and when?
  • What happens when it gets something wrong, and who fixes it?
  • Where does my customer data go, and who can see it?
  • Do I own what you build if I leave?
  • Can I turn it off in one step?

Red flags

  • A promise to replace your whole team.
  • No approval step and no log of what was sent.
  • A long contract before you have seen it work on one job.
  • Results they cannot show you from a real business.

Questions

How do I start using AI agents in my business?

Pick one repeated job, like answering new inquiries or following up on leads. Count how it goes today. Run an agent on just that job with a person approving what it sends. After 30 days, compare the numbers, then add the next job.

What is the difference between an AI agent and a chatbot?

A chatbot answers questions in a chat window. An agent does work across your tools: it reads the inquiry, writes the reply, books the time, updates your CRM and reports what it did.

What should a small business automate with AI agents first?

Work that repeats, has a clear right answer, and loses money when it is late: after-hours inquiries, lead follow-up, appointment booking and reminders, review requests, and keeping records current.

Will AI agents replace my employees?

They should not. Agents take the repeated tasks. Your people keep the relationships, the judgment calls and the final say on what reaches a customer.
